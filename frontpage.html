Lead — The War in Ukraine

Ukraine strikes deep into Russia, damaging five jets at Savasleyka airbase

Ukrainian forces struck the Savasleyka airbase in Russia’s Nizhny Novgorod region, damaging three MiG-31 jets and two Su-34 fighter-bombers, Kyiv said. Ukrainian drones also set two Rosneft oil refineries — Syzran and Kuibyshev — ablaze in the Samara region.

Europe

Energy grid hit again. Russian strikes damaged Ukrainian power and gas infrastructure, cutting electricity across five regions, Kyiv said.

Drone toll. Ukraine says its air defences downed 88 of 112 Russian drones launched overnight.

Poland’s call. Warsaw urged NATO to intercept Russian missiles over Ukraine before they cross into Polish airspace.

Rutte’s warning. NATO’s secretary general urged Europe to prepare for the possibility of extensive armed conflict.

Estonia seals border. Tallinn sealed part of its border with Russia, citing the risks on its eastern frontier.

Nuclear-test inquiry. Britain will open an independent inquiry into the effects of nuclear testing on veterans and their families.

Puigdemont to return. Carles Puigdemont will return to Spain on 12 December, ending nearly a decade of exile after courts shelved his case (Reuters).

Paris backlash. France’s government faces significant backlash over plans to deploy troops for Ukraine, reports say.

UK–EU treaties. London and Brussels are set to publish two post-Brexit treaties covering food trade and emissions trading, with legislation to enact most of the deal.

Poundland collapse. Poundland entered administration, putting 12,000 jobs at risk, after its US owner Gordon Brothers rejected at least two final bids (BBC).

EU tech fund. EU officials plan a €50 billion technology fund to rival the US and China in the quantum race, reports say.

Middle East

Netanyahu case. Judges in the prime minister’s corruption trial recommended prosecutors drop the key Case 4000 bribery charge.

October 7 inquiry. A Knesset committee approved a state commission to investigate the 7 October attacks, despite Netanyahu’s resistance.

Riyadh tower hit again. A Houthi missile struck Riyadh’s PIF Tower for a second time in a week, wounding 11, Saudi civil defence said.

Yemen exchange. The Saudi-led coalition struck Hodeidah port and targets near Sanaa, and said it intercepted missiles over Riyadh and two drones.

Oil climbs. Crude rose after the Houthis claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia’s largest refinery; Riyadh said it downed four drones over Ras Tanura.

Gaza strike. An Israeli strike in Gaza City’s Shati camp killed Hamas security official Mohammad Majed Abu al-Ouf, his wife and two daughters, 15 and 13.

Flood warning. Gaza municipalities warn winter rains could flood about 1,000 camps and shelter sites for displaced people.

Hasakah attack. An ISIL suicide bombing at a checkpoint in Hasakah, north-east Syria, killed two security personnel and wounded five.

Iran war funds. President Trump said Washington has funding to sustain its war against Iran until the end of January.

Hormuz mines. Iran reported two ‘rogue’ oil tankers hit naval mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz.

Executions reported. Iranian authorities plan to execute two anti-regime protesters at dawn after three days on death row, reports say.

Asia-Pacific

First aboard Tiangong. Pakistan’s Muhammad Zeeshan Ali became the first foreign astronaut to board China’s Tiangong station, and the first Pakistani in orbit.

Pyongyang shuts Swiss office. North Korea threatened to close Switzerland’s newly reopened office in Pyongyang, in a sanctions row over a youth camp.

Malaysia sues Google. Kuala Lumpur is suing Google for $146 million over gambling, scam and dangerous content on YouTube.

Pacific pact. Australia and Solomon Islands signed a security treaty affirming no foreign military base in the Pacific nation — a blow to China.

Indonesia mine flood. Rescuers in West Java resumed the search for 13 miners trapped in a flooded illegal gold mine near Bogor; 13 are already confirmed dead after heavy rain inundated the shaft on Monday (Reuters).

Africa

Egypt inferno. At least 30 died and 15 were wounded when a petroleum tanker overturned and burst into flames on Cairo’s Ring Road.

Nigeria meth bust. Agents seized 1,512 kg of meth worth about $227m from an industrial-scale lab in Enugu, arresting five Mexican nationals (AP).

Zambia protest ban. Authorities banned a protest against moves to scrap presidential term limits, citing a risk of clashes.

Senegal champions again. The Court of Arbitration for Sport restored Senegal as Africa Cup of Nations champions, upholding the 1–0 final win over Morocco.

Mugabe warrant. A court issued an arrest warrant for a son of ex-president Robert Mugabe after he failed to appear, reports say.

Sudan drone strikes. Drone strikes blamed on the paramilitary RSF killed 19 civilians and wounded 20 in Blue Nile state, a Sudanese rights group said (Reuters).

Americas

Parade postponed. The 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Parade was postponed to Saturday — a first — as a nor’easter hit New York (Reuters).

Cartagena hostages freed. Colombian police stormed a supermarket and freed about 20 hostages held 17 hours after a failed cash-truck robbery.

Mississippi crash. Six people, including two children, died when a small aircraft crashed near the Mississippi coast.

Power contract voided. Puerto Rico’s Supreme Court ruled 5–4 to cancel the Luma Energy power contract, giving officials a year to decide next steps.

Deportation saga ends. Kilmar Ábrego García, whose deportation to El Salvador sparked a bitter immigration fight, is leaving the US for Spain.

Iowa detonation. The US Army detonated nearly 10,000 lb of deteriorating lead azide at its Iowa ammunition plant, after court clearance.

Children returned. Washington and Moscow announced nine Ukrainian children returned to their families, in a Melania Trump-backed process.

California king tides. Forecasters warned of major coastal flooding as king tides peaked over Thanksgiving, with water above 8 feet in places and flooding already reported in the San Francisco Bay Area (BBC).

Markets & Tech

Asia tech rout. The AI trade unwound: Nikkei 225 −2.78% to 57,011.62, Kospi −6.2%, Taiex −4.8%; SoftBank slid ~15%, Nvidia whipsawed.

India tariffs. India is among 14 economies facing Section 301 duties up to 25% — 17% with a trade deal; New Delhi rejects the finding.

Trump tariffs widen. The president proposed further tariffs, including on China and the European Union, dominating US front pages.

S&P warns on Nvidia. S&P Global said Nvidia’s AA credit rating could be cut within 90 days over governance risk after Jensen Huang’s ouster and reinstatement as chief executive (CNBC).

ElevenLabs funding. Voice-AI startup ElevenLabs is reportedly in early talks to raise funds at a $33 billion valuation (Bloomberg).

Science, Space & Sport

COP31 collapse. The UN climate summit in Türkiye ended without an agreement — the first failed COP since Copenhagen in 2009; delegates called it the worst ever.

El Niño warning. An extreme El Niño is fuelling fears for the Amazon after weeks of severe weather, reports say.

UK seasons shift. Britain’s seasons are shifting: winter frost days ran about a quarter lower in the decade to 2025 than in 1961–1990, while days reaching 25°C more than doubled, the Met Office said (BBC).

Nuclear satellite fears. NATO services concluded Russia may launch a nuclear-armed satellite, ‘Sputnik-S’, this year; Rutte vowed defence in space (Reuters).

Euclid quasar. ESA’s Euclid telescope found the most distant quasar yet seen — as it was 630 million years after the Big Bang, its black hole weighing 360 million solar masses.

Europa Clipper flyby. NASA’s Europa Clipper will swing within about 3,140–3,450 km of Earth on 3 December, using the flyby to gain speed toward Jupiter.

Baby aboard the ISS. Canadian astronaut Joshua Kutryk welcomed his third child, a daughter, while aboard the International Space Station (BBC).

Davis Cup. Holders Italy edged South Korea 2–1 in Bologna, Berrettini and Vavassori winning the deciding doubles in two tiebreaks (Reuters).

Spain sweat on Alcaraz. Spain face Austria in the Davis Cup Final 8, with Carlos Alcaraz still building fitness after injury.

Chess title match. World champion Gukesh and challenger Sindarov drew their title-match opener in 38 moves near Geneva.

Champions League. Real Madrid came from 1–0 down to rout PSV 5–1 at the Bernabéu, substitute Jude Bellingham sparking the comeback.

More Champions League. Liverpool beat Club Brugge 3–0 and Bayern Munich lost for the first time this season, while Manchester United drew with Sporting in a chaotic match.
